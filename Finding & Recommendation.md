## Key Findings
![Monthly MRR movement](output/charts/02_mrr_movement.png)

![Gross vs net revenue retention](output/charts/04_grr_vs_nrr.png)

### 1. Overall MRR grew, but recurring revenue performance was volatile

MRR increased from approximately $38.0K in January to $40.7K by
September. However, the company experienced several periods of negative
monthly growth, including a 6.1% decline in July.

The MRR movement bridge showed that the deterioration was driven primarily
by contraction and churn rather than changes in new customer acquisition.

### 2. Mid-Market showed the greatest retention volatility

Mid-Market NRR fell below 80% in multiple months as several customers
contracted or churned. Customer-level drilldowns showed that a relatively
small number of accounts generated a meaningful share of the revenue loss.

This suggests that Finance and Customer Success should evaluate large
contraction events separately from broad segment-level trends.

### 3. Late-period growth was driven primarily by reactivation

MRR recovered in August and September, but the recovery was driven largely
by previously churned customers returning rather than new customer
acquisition or expansion.

This demonstrates why total MRR growth alone can provide an incomplete view
of recurring revenue health.

### 4. Enterprise remained the largest and most stable revenue base

Enterprise represented the majority of company MRR and experienced no full
customer churn during the analysis period. However, several large
contractions demonstrate that individual account movements can still have a
material company-level impact.

## Recommendations

- Prioritize high-value customers showing contraction for Customer Success
  review before full churn occurs.
- Investigate the characteristics of customers generating strong expansion
  to identify potential GTM upsell opportunities.
- Monitor MRR movement, GRR, and NRR alongside total MRR to distinguish
  sustainable existing-customer growth from acquisition or reactivation.
- Combine subscription data with product usage and pricing data in future
  analysis to investigate the underlying drivers of contraction and churn.
